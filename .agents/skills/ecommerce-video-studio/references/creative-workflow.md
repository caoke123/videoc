# Original UGC creative workflow

Use this when no reference video exists or when the job needs original ideas. The operator may supply only a product photo and a few verified selling points.

## Brief to concepts

1. Identify SKU, audience, real use occasion, verified product facts, desired emotion, platform, duration, and available evidence. Mark missing facts explicitly.
2. Draft 2–3 genuinely different concepts using concrete everyday situations. For each, write a hook, the viewer problem or desire, product role, visual proof, and natural CTA. Select the concept that can be shown truthfully with available assets.
3. Write a spoken script that fits the target duration. Keep the line conversational and leave room for natural pauses. Separate spoken words from on-screen text and claims.
4. Define the on-camera person's approximate age, everyday identity, wardrobe, manner of speech, and relationship to the product. Avoid assuming the same driver or appearance for every category. If a real person reference is supplied, preserve only the identity or traits the user authorizes.
5. Define scenes and believable actions from product use. Each action must obey the category profile and the actual product structure. No action is required just to keep the subject busy; a glance, touch, or change of framing can be enough.
6. Break the script into short shots. Give each a single visual objective and at most one main action/move. Decide whether the shot needs a product photo, person image, installed/attached-state image, native dialogue, post TTS, or silent plate.

## Useful output in the job

Record the selected concept, rejected alternatives with brief reasons, character and scene plan, script, visual proof, reference roles, and shot plan alongside `brief.json` and `shot-plan.json`. The batch should be understandable without a competitor video. Use keyframes to test role, product scale, contact, and geography before animation.

For an 8–12 second UGC example, a workable structure is: 1–2 second hook; 2–4 second natural context; 2–3 second product interaction or already-used state; 1–2 second detail/proof; short spoken or overlaid CTA. Adjust to the actual script; do not force every function into every video.

## Review criteria

Ask whether a real customer could plausibly say and do this, whether the product is recognizable and physically correct, whether the selling point is actually demonstrated, and whether the first seconds earn attention. If a scene requires an unsupported product claim or impossible action, revise the concept before generation.
